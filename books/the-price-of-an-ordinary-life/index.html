The Price of an Ordinary Life

A Modern Indian Novel About Money, Marriage, Work, Debt, Family, and the Fight for Dignity

by Parvind Kumar

★★★★★ 4.8 / 5 (Goodreads Ratings)

A middle-class man in Delhi NCR loses his job — and a year strips away every assumption his family made about security.

The Price of an Ordinary Life is a family drama by Indian author Parvind Kumar following Raghav Sharma — not poor, not rich, an ordinary middle-class man with a home loan, two children, ageing parents, and a job he loses in the opening pages. What follows is a year of hospital bills, a credit card whose minimum due turns out to be a trap, a farmer father drowning in his own kind of debt, and a wife rediscovering the career she once set aside.

The novel follows three generations of one family — a grandfather who needed only enough to survive, a father taught to need enough to succeed, and children only beginning to ask what it means to be free — set between a North Indian village and a rapidly changing NCR.

It is a story about EMIs and dignity, about a sister who refuses a marriage proposal that reduced her to a checklist, and about how much an ordinary life actually costs when the bill finally comes due.

Table of Contents

  • Chapter 1: The Job Raghav Loses: A comfortable middle-class life begins to unravel.
  • Chapter 2: The Minimum Due: Debt, a hospital bill, and the trap hidden in fine print.
  • Chapter 3: Two Kinds of Debt: Raghav's father, a farmer in Uttar Pradesh, faces his own version of the same fight.
  • Chapter 4: What the Children Found: A ledger discovered by accident changes what the family understands about itself.
  • Chapter 5: The Job Offer: Raghav is offered the one job that could fix everything, if he becomes what once broke him.
  • Chapter 6: The Price: What the family finally decides an ordinary life is worth.
