HISTORY

Freedom and Partition

The Struggle for Independence, the Decisions Behind Partition, and the Human Cost of 1947

by Parvind Kumar

★★★★★ 4.8 / 5 (Goodreads Ratings)

How India's freedom movement led to Partition — from Gandhi's mass movements to the Radcliffe Line and the refugee crisis that followed August 1947.

Freedom and Partition is a history by Indian author Parvind Kumar tracing India's path from British colonial rule to independence — and why that journey ended in the division of the subcontinent. It follows the rise of Indian nationalism and Gandhi's mass movements, the emergence of Muhammad Ali Jinnah and the Muslim League, and the negotiations of the 1940s through the Cabinet Mission, the Mountbatten Plan, and the Radcliffe Line.

Rather than a simple story of heroes and villains, the book examines the competing ambitions, fears, and failures that brought the subcontinent to its final crossroads — and the enormous refugee crisis that followed the celebrations of August 1947.

At its center is one enduring question: how the dream of freedom became intertwined with the division of a country, and what that meant for the millions of ordinary lives transformed by 1947.

Table of Contents

  • Chapter 1: The Rise of Indian Nationalism: Gandhi's mass movements and the growing demand for self-rule.
  • Chapter 2: Jinnah and the Muslim League: The emergence of a rival political vision.
  • Chapter 3: The Cabinet Mission and Mountbatten Plan: The negotiations that shaped the final settlement.
  • Chapter 4: The Radcliffe Line: How a border was drawn, and what it failed to account for.
  • Chapter 5: August 1947: Independence, celebration, and the immediate aftermath.
  • Chapter 6: The Human Cost: The refugee crisis and the families divided by a new map.
