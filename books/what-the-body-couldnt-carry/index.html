What the Body Couldn't Carry

A Novel of Love, Loss, and Motherhood by Surrogacy

by Parvind Kumar

★★★★★ 4.8 / 5 (Goodreads Ratings)

A woman's years-long journey through infertility, grief, and an unexpected family secret that reshapes what motherhood means to her.

What the Body Couldn't Carry is a novel by Indian author Parvind Kumar following Kaveri Iyer through years of fertility treatment after her first pregnancy stops progressing — cycles of injections, blood tests, hope, and quiet, accumulating grief, kept in a private notebook only she can read. Her husband Rohan struggles too, retreating into old radios and repair work before learning that loving someone doesn't always mean fixing them.

When a diagnosis of early-stage rheumatoid arthritis forces Kaveri to confront the cost of continuing treatment, her doctor suggests gestational surrogacy — a suggestion that feels, at first, like losing the motherhood she'd always imagined. Then her mother reveals a secret kept for decades: a daughter Kaveri never knew existed.

The novel is an intimate story about marriage, infertility, and the difference between carrying a child and being a mother — and about letting go of the future imagined to make room for the one still possible.

Table of Contents

  • Chapter 1: Two Lines: Kaveri's pregnancy, and the moment it stops progressing.
  • Chapter 2: The Notebook: Years of treatment, recorded in numbers and feelings no one else fully understands.
  • Chapter 3: What Rohan Learns: Grief, old radios, and the difference between fixing and staying.
  • Chapter 4: A Diagnosis: Rheumatoid arthritis, and a cost Kaveri isn't sure she can keep paying.
  • Chapter 5: Her Mother's Secret: A daughter no one ever mentioned, and what it explains.
  • Chapter 6: What Motherhood Means: Choosing gestational surrogacy, and redefining what it means to carry a child.
