SOCIETY & RELATIONSHIPS

The New Indian Marriage

Love, Dating Apps, Live-In Relationships, Divorce, Infidelity and the Changing Indian Family

by Parvind Kumar

★★★★★ 4.8 / 5 (Goodreads Ratings)

What Indian marriage actually looks like today — arranged marriage in the smartphone era, live-in relationships, dowry, dating apps, and a generation delaying marriage entirely.

The New Indian Marriage is a nonfiction study by Indian author Parvind Kumar examining what marriage actually looks like across India today, not what it's supposed to look like. Each chapter opens with a real story and is grounded in a Supreme Court ruling, a national survey, or a government dataset, with fifteen charts and a full source list backing the claims.

The book covers arranged marriage in the smartphone era, dowry and why the law hasn't stopped it, inter-caste and interfaith love still fought over in court, live-in couples with nowhere to legally rent, dating apps quietly becoming marriage bureaus, financial independence and the resentment it can trigger, and divorce, infidelity, surrogacy and LGBTQ+ rights.

It doesn't argue that arranged marriage is outdated or love marriage is better. It shows, honestly, what marriage in India looks like now — messy, uneven, and still somehow holding a country together.

Table of Contents

  • Chapter 1: Arranged Marriage in the Smartphone Era: WhatsApp introductions and the persistence of family-arranged matches.
  • Chapter 2: Dowry, Sixty Years Later: Why a decades-old law hasn't ended the practice.
  • Chapter 3: Love Across the Lines: Inter-caste and interfaith marriage, still fought over in court.
  • Chapter 4: Live-In and Legally Invisible: Couples navigating a country with no clear rental or legal status for cohabitation.
  • Chapter 5: Dating Apps as Marriage Bureaus: How swiping quietly became a modern matchmaking system.
  • Chapter 6: Divorce, Infidelity and What Comes Next: Surrogacy, LGBTQ+ rights, and a generation delaying marriage.
