SIKH HISTORY & FAITH

Panja Sahib

The Sacred Handprint of Guru Nanak and the Story of Faith, Service, Courage, and Sacrifice

by Parvind Kumar

★★★★★ 4.8 / 5 (Goodreads Ratings)

The story of Gurdwara Panja Sahib at Hasan Abdal — Guru Nanak's visit, the sacred handprint, and the courage of Saka Panja Sahib in 1922.

Panja Sahib is a history and faith title by Indian author Parvind Kumar on one of the most significant Sikh pilgrimage sites, located in Hasan Abdal, Pakistan. It retells the traditional account of Guru Nanak's visit with Bhai Mardana and Wali Qandhari — a spring, a stone bearing the impression of a hand and a lesson in humility.

The book also traces the historical development of the gurdwara, the Gurdwara Reform Movement of the early twentieth century, and Saka Panja Sahib — the 1922 railway episode remembered for the courage and sacrifice of Bhai Karam Singh and Bhai Partap Singh. It reflects on seva, equality and compassion and on why Panja Sahib remains important to Sikh communities across borders.

Table of Contents

  • Chapter 1: Guru Nanak at Hasan Abdal: The traditional narrative of the visit.
  • Chapter 2: The Sacred Handprint: The spiritual significance of the Panja in Sikh devotion.
  • Chapter 3: Bhai Mardana and Wali Qandhari: Companionship, humility and transformation.
  • Chapter 4: The History of the Gurdwara: Development and importance of the sacred site.
  • Chapter 5: Reform and Saka Panja Sahib: The Gurdwara Reform Movement and the events of 1922.
  • Chapter 6: Seva and Heritage: Selfless service, equality and Sikh heritage across borders.
