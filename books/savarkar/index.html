HISTORY & POLITICS

Savarkar

The Revolutionary, the Ideologue, and the Controversial Legacy of Modern India

by Parvind Kumar

★★★★★ 4.8 / 5 (Goodreads Ratings)

The life and ideas of Vinayak Damodar Savarkar — revolutionary, Hindutva ideologue and one of modern India's most debated figures.

Savarkar is a biography by Indian author Parvind Kumar on Vinayak Damodar Savarkar, a revolutionary nationalist, prolific writer and central figure in debates over Hindutva whose legacy continues to divide opinion. Born in 1883 in Bhagur, Maharashtra, he moved from early revolutionary activity to London, where he joined networks of Indian nationalists, before his arrest, trial and imprisonment in the Cellular Jail in the Andamans.

The book examines the origins and arguments of Hindutva, his views on nationalism and citizenship and his efforts against caste discrimination and untouchability, as well as the controversies — his mercy petitions, his political positions in the late colonial period and the investigations surrounding Gandhi's assassination. It encourages readers to examine documents and context rather than slogans.

Table of Contents

  • Chapter 1: Bhagur to London: Childhood, education and early revolutionary activity.
  • Chapter 2: Arrest and the Cellular Jail: Trial, imprisonment and mercy petitions.
  • Chapter 3: The Revolt of 1857: His interpretation of the 1857 uprising.
  • Chapter 4: Hindutva: Origins and arguments of his political thought.
  • Chapter 5: Politics and Social Reform: Nationalism, citizenship and his stand on untouchability.
  • Chapter 6: Controversies and Legacy: Debated episodes and the distinction between fact and dispute.
